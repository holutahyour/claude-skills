# Editorial Review Rubric (Stage A)

Sources: Kirkus Reviews, Publishers Weekly/BookLife, Foreword/Clarion
Reviews, BlueInk Review, IndieReader, Library Journal, Reedsy editorial
assessment practice. Each dimension below names which outlet's practice it
is adapted from.

## Scoring scale

Anchored 1-5 (adapted from IndieReader, which explicitly judges indie books
against all books, not on a curve for self-publishing). Half-points allowed.

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| 1 | Really bad |
| 2 | Mediocre — one or two bright spots |
| 3 | Good — worth reading |
| 4 | Very good |
| 5 | Excellent — a must-read |

**Rule: any score below 4 must cite a concrete example from the actual
manuscript text plus one actionable fix.** (Library Journal requires
reviewers to back criticism with specifics — the same standard applies to
scoring here.)

## Common dimensions (both genres)

- **Writing quality** — prose control, sentence rhythm, voice consistency
  across the manuscript. (Publishers Weekly/BookLife: "quality of writing.")
- **Structure & pacing** — logical progression, escalating value chapter to
  chapter, no padding, no orphaned or redundant sections. (Foreword/Clarion:
  "plot, pacing"; Reedsy: does the structure work and make sense to the
  reader.)
- **Originality** — not interchangeable with competing titles in the same
  category; a genuine angle. (PW/BookLife: "originality of ideas" and
  "overall uniqueness within its category.")
- **Audience fit** — actually serves the stated reader and their specific
  need. (Library Journal's test: "who the book best serves.")
- **Production polish** — front/back matter present and complete, heading
  levels consistent (H1 chapter / H2 sub-section, no H3/H4), no visible
  AI-pattern tells (stock names like Sarah/Tom/Emma/John, "Let me share an
  example...", "Consider this scenario...", filler transitions). (Foreword/
  Clarion treats "packaging" as a first-class, separate criterion from prose
  quality — polish is judged on its own, not folded into writing quality.)

## Fiction-specific dimensions

(Reedsy developmental-editing / editorial-assessment practice)

- **Character arc & intentionality** — are characters purposefully drawn
  (likeable or unlikeable *on purpose*), do they actually change across the
  book, or is characterization accidental/flat?
- **Show-don't-tell execution** — concrete scene-work vs. summarized
  narration where a scene was called for.
- **POV consistency** — no unintentional head-hopping or tense drift.
- **Dialogue naturalness** — reads as speech, not exposition delivery.
- **Hook strength** — do the opening pages earn the reader's continued
  attention.
- **Earned resolution** — does the ending follow from what was set up, not
  a convenient shortcut.

## Nonfiction-specific dimensions

(Reedsy nonfiction checks + Library Journal's substance/execution split)

- **Argument/thesis clarity** — is the book's central claim stated and
  followed through, not just implied.
- **Evidence & factual accuracy** — spot-check specific claims (names,
  dates, figures, quotations) against what's verifiable; flag anything that
  reads as invented or unverifiable presented as fact.
- **Actionable value** — does the reader leave with something they can use,
  not just information restated.
- **Completeness** — is anything conspicuously missing: a related concept,
  an underexplained idea, a missing example, a missing practical takeaway.
  (Reedsy: nonfiction assessments explicitly check for *absence*, not just
  what's present.)

## Stage A close-out

After scoring all dimensions, write a 150-250 word critic's-voice summary:
context for the book (what it is, who it's for) followed by a concise,
unbiased verdict — positive, negative, or mixed (Kirkus's own framing of
"most likely, mixed"). This is prose, in the voice of a professional
capsule review, not another table.
